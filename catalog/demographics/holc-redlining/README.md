# HOLC Redlining Grades (1930s)

The 1930s Home Owners' Loan Corporation (HOLC) "residential security" grades for St. Louis, from the University of Richmond's Mapping Inequality project, which says of the source maps: "These maps, created in the 1930s, were used to grade the 'security' of real estate investments, leading to the practice known as redlining." Each polygon carries its grade — A ("Best"), B ("Still Desirable"), C ("Definitely Declining"), D ("Hazardous") — along with the neighborhood label from the original map sheet and the map's own fill color. Areas graded D were systematically denied mortgage capital, and the D-grade footprint remains one of the strongest predictors of present-day disinvestment; joining these 127 polygons against this catalog's parcel, vacancy, LRA land-bank and ACS income data shows that persistence directly.
Unlike the city's own datasets in this catalog, this is **not** City of St. Louis data: it is historical scholarship published by Mapping Inequality (Digital Scholarship Lab, University of Richmond), extracted for St. Louis from the project's national dataset as mirrored on Source Cooperative, and licensed CC BY-NC-SA 4.0 — the one collection in this catalog with a non-commercial license. Citation: Robert K. Nelson, LaDale Winling, et al., "Mapping Inequality," American Panorama, https://dsl.richmond.edu/panorama/redlining/. Explore it in the [St. Louis data browser](https://cholmes.github.io/stlouis-data-browser/#/demographics/holc-redlining/collection.json).
